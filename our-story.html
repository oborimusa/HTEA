From The Security Post
To The General Manager's Desk

"I didn't learn hospitality in a classroom. I learned it from the ground up."

Obori Dahiru

Founder, HTEA & Drecot Consults

🛡️

Lagos: The Foundation

38 years ago, my journey into the Nigerian hospitality industry began—not in a suit, but in a security uniform. I started as a Security Guard at a hotel in Lagos. I stood at the gate, watched the guests arrive, and observed how the front desk handled them.

I didn't just watch; I learned. I learned that hospitality is discipline. It is the details—the way a door is opened, the way a complaint is handled. Lagos taught me that the guest experience starts from the moment they step onto the property, long before they reach the room.

🎪

Jos: Events & The People

My journey took me to Jos, where I transitioned into event promotion and hospitality management. Organizing events in a new city taught me the power of human connection. I learned how to manage large crowds, coordinate vendors, and handle high-pressure situations.

But it was also in Jos that I saw the first signs of the "Brain Drain" problem. I trained young men and women to manage events, set up stages, and serve guests. As soon as they became highly skilled, a bigger promoter would offer them ₦10k more, and they would leave. I learned that training without a retention strategy is a loss to the business.

👔

Ilorin: Leadership & The Pain of Loss

In Ilorin, I rose through the ranks—from Admin/HR Officer to Manager, and eventually to Deputy General Manager (Admin). I was no longer just observing; I was running the operations. I was responsible for hiring, training, and retaining staff.

"In Ilorin, I trained 23 of my staff. Within one year, 18 of them left for a competitor because of a ₦10,000 salary increase. I felt the pain of losing my best hands to the market. I realized that paper certificates and loyalty pledges do not stop staff from leaving. Owners need a system."

I saw hotel owners spend millions on recruitment and training, only to lose their investment. I saw staff struggle without proper certification to advance their careers. The industry was stuck in a cycle of high turnover and low standards.

💻

Osogbo: The Tech Solution

Today, I am a Software Engineer. I didn't just complain about the problem; I taught myself how to build the solution. After completing my software engineering course, I founded OneStack Digital Agency—a tech company that builds websites, booking systems, and automation for Nigerian hotels, restaurants, and SMEs.

As I built tech solutions for these businesses, I quickly realized that software alone wasn't enough. The biggest problem was still human capital. So, I founded HospitalityRecruit to bridge the gap between skilled talent and quality hotels.

The HTEA HR System was born out of this 38-year experience. It is the only system designed to solve the Nigerian "Train & Run" problem. We don't use paper certificates. We use a digital QR verification platform. We don't just certify your staff; we track their retention and replace them if they leave. We are turning a retention crisis into a talent ecosystem.

🏆

The Mission

HTEA Osun 2027 is not just an award show. It is the launchpad for a national movement. We are celebrating the owners who built the industry, and we are certifying the staff who keep it running.

After Osun, we are taking this to Oyo, Ekiti, Kwara, and eventually Abuja. We are building the infrastructure for a world-class Nigerian hospitality sector.

Lagos taught me discipline. Jos taught me events and people. Ilorin taught me leadership and retention. Osun is where I bring it all together.
